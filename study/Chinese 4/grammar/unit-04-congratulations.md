# Chinese 4 · Unit 4: 祝贺 (Zhùhè) — Congratulations

**Grammar & Sentence Patterns** · source: PDF pp. 63, 70–71, 75–79 (printed pp. 47, 54–55, 59–63)

This unit is about congratulating people and responding modestly. Grammar: (1) 其实 (qíshí) “actually / in fact” (correcting an impression); (2) 明明 (míngmíng) “clearly, obviously” (stressing a fact that's being ignored); (3) 怪不得 (guàibude) “no wonder”. Answers marked “model answer” were written for this sheet.

## 重点语句 (Zhòngdiǎn yǔjù) — Key sentences

1. <ruby>其实<rt>Qíshí</rt></ruby><ruby>我<rt>wǒ</rt></ruby><ruby>只是<rt>zhǐshì</rt></ruby><ruby>运气<rt>yùnqi</rt></ruby><ruby>比较<rt>bǐjiào</rt></ruby><ruby>好<rt>hǎo</rt></ruby><ruby>罢了<rt>bàle</rt></ruby>。  
   Actually, I was just rather lucky, that's all.

2. <ruby>明明<rt>Míngmíng</rt></ruby><ruby>是<rt>shì</rt></ruby><ruby>实力<rt>shílì</rt></ruby><ruby>强<rt>qiáng</rt></ruby>，<ruby>哪儿<rt>nǎr</rt></ruby><ruby>是<rt>shì</rt></ruby><ruby>因为<rt>yīnwèi</rt></ruby><ruby>运气<rt>yùnqi</rt></ruby><ruby>啊<rt>a</rt></ruby>！  
   It's obviously because you're good — luck had nothing to do with it!

3. <ruby>哪里<rt>Nǎli</rt></ruby>，<ruby>哪里<rt>nǎli</rt></ruby>，<ruby>你<rt>nǐ</rt></ruby><ruby>过奖<rt>guòjiǎng</rt></ruby><ruby>了<rt>le</rt></ruby>。  
   Oh no, not at all — you flatter me.

4. <ruby>怪不得<rt>Guàibude</rt></ruby><ruby>你<rt>nǐ</rt></ruby><ruby>今天<rt>jīntiān</rt></ruby><ruby>这么<rt>zhème</rt></ruby><ruby>开心<rt>kāixīn</rt></ruby>，<ruby>原来<rt>yuánlái</rt></ruby><ruby>是<rt>shì</rt></ruby><ruby>双喜<rt>shuāngxǐ</rt></ruby><ruby>临门<rt>línmén</rt></ruby><ruby>啊<rt>a</rt></ruby>！  
   No wonder you're so happy today — it's a double celebration!

## 语法点注释 (Yǔfǎ diǎn zhùshì) — Grammar notes

### 1. 其实 (qíshí) — The usage of 其实 (qíshí)

<ruby>副词<rt>Fùcí</rt></ruby>“<ruby>其实<rt>qíshí</rt></ruby>”<ruby>表示<rt>biǎoshì</rt></ruby><ruby>所说<rt>suǒshuō</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>情况<rt>qíngkuàng</rt></ruby><ruby>是<rt>shì</rt></ruby><ruby>真<rt>zhēn</rt></ruby><ruby>实<rt>shí</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby>。<ruby>用<rt>Yòng</rt></ruby><ruby>在<rt>zài</rt></ruby><ruby>动词<rt>dòngcí</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>qián</rt></ruby><ruby>或<rt>huò</rt></ruby><ruby>主语<rt>zhǔyǔ</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>qián</rt></ruby><ruby>承接<rt>chéngjiē</rt></ruby><ruby>上<rt>shàng</rt></ruby><ruby>文<rt>wén</rt></ruby>，<ruby>常<rt>cháng</rt></ruby><ruby>引出<rt>yǐnchū</rt></ruby><ruby>与<rt>yǔ</rt></ruby><ruby>上<rt>shàng</rt></ruby><ruby>文<rt>wén</rt></ruby><ruby>相反<rt>xiāngfǎn</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>意思<rt>yìsi</rt></ruby>，<ruby>表示<rt>biǎoshì</rt></ruby><ruby>对<rt>duì</rt></ruby><ruby>上<rt>shàng</rt></ruby><ruby>文<rt>wén</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>更<rt>gèng</rt></ruby><ruby>正<rt>zhèng</rt></ruby><ruby>或<rt>huò</rt></ruby><ruby>补充<rt>bǔchōng</rt></ruby>。<ruby>例如<rt>Lìrú</rt></ruby>：  
The adverb “其实 (qíshí)” marks what's said as the real situation. Placed before the verb or the subject, it follows on from what came before and often introduces the opposite — correcting or adding to it, e.g.,

> **Pattern:** （<ruby>表面<rt>biǎomiàn</rt></ruby>／<ruby>别人<rt>biérén</rt></ruby><ruby>以为<rt>yǐwéi</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>情况<rt>qíngkuàng</rt></ruby>），<ruby>其实<rt>qíshí</rt></ruby> + <ruby>真实<rt>zhēnshí</rt></ruby><ruby>情况<rt>qíngkuàng</rt></ruby>  
> (how it looks / what people think), actually + (the truth)

1. <ruby>她<rt>Tā</rt></ruby><ruby>看<rt>kàn</rt></ruby><ruby>起来<rt>qǐlai</rt></ruby><ruby>只有<rt>zhǐyǒu</rt></ruby><ruby>二十<rt>èrshí</rt></ruby><ruby>多<rt>duō</rt></ruby><ruby>岁<rt>suì</rt></ruby>，<ruby>其实<rt>qíshí</rt></ruby><ruby>她<rt>tā</rt></ruby><ruby>已经<rt>yǐjīng</rt></ruby><ruby>快<rt>kuài</rt></ruby><ruby>四十<rt>sìshí</rt></ruby><ruby>了<rt>le</rt></ruby>。  
   She looks only twenty-something, but she's actually nearly forty.

2. <ruby>你们<rt>Nǐmen</rt></ruby><ruby>只<rt>zhǐ</rt></ruby><ruby>知道<rt>zhīdào</rt></ruby><ruby>他<rt>tā</rt></ruby><ruby>会<rt>huì</rt></ruby><ruby>说<rt>shuō</rt></ruby><ruby>英语<rt>Yīngyǔ</rt></ruby>，<ruby>其实<rt>qíshí</rt></ruby><ruby>他<rt>tā</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>法语<rt>Fǎyǔ</rt></ruby><ruby>也<rt>yě</rt></ruby><ruby>很<rt>hěn</rt></ruby><ruby>好<rt>hǎo</rt></ruby>。  
   You only know he speaks English — actually his French is good too.

3. <ruby>他<rt>Tā</rt></ruby><ruby>说<rt>shuō</rt></ruby><ruby>他<rt>tā</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>成绩<rt>chéngjì</rt></ruby><ruby>不<rt>bú</rt></ruby><ruby>太<rt>tài</rt></ruby><ruby>好<rt>hǎo</rt></ruby>，<ruby>其实<rt>qíshí</rt></ruby><ruby>他<rt>tā</rt></ruby><ruby>是<rt>shì</rt></ruby><ruby>第一<rt>dì-yī</rt></ruby><ruby>名<rt>míng</rt></ruby>。  
   He says his grades aren't great, but actually he came first.

> 💡 Appearance first, 其实 (qíshí) + reality second: ✓ 那家公司看起来很小 (nà jiā gōngsī kàn qǐlai hěn xiǎo)，其实实力很强 (qíshí shílì hěn qiáng). Reversing it (✗ 其实实力很强 (qíshí shílì hěn qiáng)，看起来很小 (kàn qǐlai hěn xiǎo)) breaks the logic.

### 2. 明明 (míngmíng) — The usage of 明明 (míngmíng)

<ruby>副词<rt>Fùcí</rt></ruby>“<ruby>明明<rt>míngmíng</rt></ruby>”<ruby>意思<rt>yìsi</rt></ruby><ruby>是<rt>shì</rt></ruby>“<ruby>显然<rt>xiǎnrán</rt></ruby>、<ruby>清楚<rt>qīngchu</rt></ruby>；<ruby>确实<rt>quèshí</rt></ruby>”，<ruby>表示<rt>biǎoshì</rt></ruby><ruby>显然<rt>xiǎnrán</rt></ruby><ruby>如此<rt>rúcǐ</rt></ruby>，<ruby>用以<rt>yòngyǐ</rt></ruby><ruby>强调<rt>qiángdiào</rt></ruby><ruby>事实<rt>shìshí</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>确定性<rt>quèdìngxìng</rt></ruby>。<ruby>有时<rt>Yǒushí</rt></ruby><ruby>用<rt>yòng</rt></ruby>“<ruby>明明<rt>míngmíng</rt></ruby>”<ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>小<rt>xiǎo</rt></ruby><ruby>句<rt>jù</rt></ruby><ruby>前边<rt>qiánbian</rt></ruby><ruby>或<rt>huò</rt></ruby><ruby>后边<rt>hòubian</rt></ruby><ruby>有<rt>yǒu</rt></ruby><ruby>表示<rt>biǎoshì</rt></ruby><ruby>反问<rt>fǎnwèn</rt></ruby><ruby>或<rt>huò</rt></ruby><ruby>转折<rt>zhuǎnzhé</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>小<rt>xiǎo</rt></ruby><ruby>句<rt>jù</rt></ruby>。<ruby>例如<rt>Lìrú</rt></ruby>：  
The adverb “明明 (míngmíng)” means “obviously, clearly, really”: it stresses that something is plainly a fact. The clause with 明明 (míngmíng) is often followed (or preceded) by a rhetorical question or a contrast, e.g.,

> **Pattern:** <ruby>明明<rt>míngmíng</rt></ruby> + <ruby>事实<rt>shìshí</rt></ruby>，（<ruby>可是<rt>kěshì</rt></ruby>／<ruby>却<rt>què</rt></ruby>）+ <ruby>相反<rt>xiāngfǎn</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>情况<rt>qíngkuàng</rt></ruby>／<ruby>反问<rt>fǎnwèn</rt></ruby>  
> obviously + (fact), (but) + (something that doesn't fit) / rhetorical question

1. <ruby>这<rt>Zhè</rt></ruby><ruby>件<rt>jiàn</rt></ruby><ruby>衣服<rt>yīfu</rt></ruby><ruby>明明<rt>míngmíng</rt></ruby><ruby>很<rt>hěn</rt></ruby><ruby>漂亮<rt>piàoliang</rt></ruby>，<ruby>你<rt>nǐ</rt></ruby><ruby>怎么<rt>zěnme</rt></ruby><ruby>不<rt>bù</rt></ruby><ruby>喜欢<rt>xǐhuan</rt></ruby><ruby>呢<rt>ne</rt></ruby>？  
   This dress is obviously beautiful — how come you don't like it?

2. <ruby>这<rt>Zhè</rt></ruby><ruby>次<rt>cì</rt></ruby><ruby>考试<rt>kǎoshì</rt></ruby><ruby>明明<rt>míngmíng</rt></ruby><ruby>很<rt>hěn</rt></ruby><ruby>难<rt>nán</rt></ruby>，<ruby>可是<rt>kěshì</rt></ruby><ruby>大家<rt>dàjiā</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>成绩<rt>chéngjì</rt></ruby><ruby>却<rt>què</rt></ruby><ruby>很<rt>hěn</rt></ruby><ruby>好<rt>hǎo</rt></ruby>。  
   This exam was clearly hard, yet everyone did well.

> 💡 The second half must clash with the fact: ✓ 他明明获得了冠军 (tā míngmíng huòdé le guànjūn)，为什么看起来不太开心呢 (wèishénme kàn qǐlai bú tài kāixīn ne)？ ✓ 我明明告诉他了 (wǒ míngmíng gàosu tā le)，他怎么会不知道呢 (tā zěnme huì bù zhīdào ne)？

### 3. 怪不得 (guàibude) — The usage of 怪不得 (guàibude)

“<ruby>怪不得<rt>Guàibude</rt></ruby>”<ruby>表示<rt>biǎoshì</rt></ruby><ruby>明白<rt>míngbai</rt></ruby><ruby>了<rt>le</rt></ruby><ruby>原因<rt>yuányīn</rt></ruby>，<ruby>不再<rt>búzài</rt></ruby><ruby>觉得<rt>juéde</rt></ruby><ruby>奇怪<rt>qíguài</rt></ruby>，<ruby>含有<rt>hányǒu</rt></ruby><ruby>突然<rt>tūrán</rt></ruby><ruby>醒悟<rt>xǐngwù</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>意思<rt>yìsi</rt></ruby>。<ruby>前后<rt>Qiánhòu</rt></ruby><ruby>常有<rt>chángyǒu</rt></ruby><ruby>说明<rt>shuōmíng</rt></ruby><ruby>原因<rt>yuányīn</rt></ruby><ruby>的<rt>de</rt></ruby><ruby>句子<rt>jùzi</rt></ruby>。<ruby>例如<rt>Lìrú</rt></ruby>：  
“怪不得 (guàibude)” means you've understood the reason, so something no longer seems strange — a sudden realisation. A clause giving the reason usually comes before or after it, e.g.,

> **Pattern:** （<ruby>原因<rt>yuányīn</rt></ruby>），<ruby>怪不得<rt>guàibude</rt></ruby> + <ruby>现象<rt>xiànxiàng</rt></ruby>　·　<ruby>怪不得<rt>guàibude</rt></ruby> + <ruby>现象<rt>xiànxiàng</rt></ruby>，（<ruby>原因<rt>yuányīn</rt></ruby>）  
> (reason), no wonder + (the odd thing) · no wonder + (odd thing), (reason)

1. <ruby>他<rt>Tā</rt></ruby><ruby>在<rt>zài</rt></ruby><ruby>中国<rt>Zhōngguó</rt></ruby><ruby>生活了<rt>shēnghuóle</rt></ruby><ruby>三<rt>sān</rt></ruby><ruby>年<rt>nián</rt></ruby>，<ruby>怪不得<rt>guàibude</rt></ruby><ruby>他<rt>tā</rt></ruby><ruby>对<rt>duì</rt></ruby><ruby>中国<rt>Zhōngguó</rt></ruby><ruby>那么<rt>nàme</rt></ruby><ruby>了解<rt>liǎojiě</rt></ruby>。  
   He lived in China for three years — no wonder he knows it so well.

2. <ruby>怪不得<rt>Guàibude</rt></ruby><ruby>这么<rt>zhème</rt></ruby><ruby>冷<rt>lěng</rt></ruby>，<ruby>我<rt>wǒ</rt></ruby><ruby>忘了<rt>wàngle</rt></ruby><ruby>关<rt>guān</rt></ruby><ruby>空调<rt>kōngtiáo</rt></ruby><ruby>了<rt>le</rt></ruby>。  
   No wonder it's so cold — I forgot to turn off the air conditioning.

> 💡 怪不得 (guàibude) goes in front of the thing that puzzled you, not the reason: ✓ 怪不得房间里这么热 (guàibude fángjiān li zhème rè)，我忘了开空调了 (wǒ wàng le kāi kōngtiáo le). ✗ 房间里这么热 (fángjiān li zhème rè)，怪不得我忘了开空调了 (guàibude wǒ wàng le kāi kōngtiáo le).
